03 — Method

How Den Works

The feedback describes a repeatable working method rather than a set of isolated skills. It moves from understanding, through engineering, to capability that outlives the original task. Expand each stage for detail.

01Understand

Den attempts to understand the underlying problem rather than immediately treating the visible symptom. The feedback describes a disciplined approach: structured investigation, problem decomposition, and a genuine interest in how the system actually behaves before anything is changed.

  • Root-cause analysis before remediation
  • Structured investigation and problem decomposition
  • Understanding system behaviour as a whole, not just the failing component
02Analyse

Complex problems are broken into components. Dependencies are identified, constraints are made explicit, and the practical solution space is narrowed deliberately rather than by guesswork.

  • Breaking complex problems into manageable components
  • Identifying dependencies and constraints
  • Finding practical solutions that hold in production
Reported in 360 feedback: reviewers describe Den's ability to “root out the causes of problems”.
03Plan

Planning happens before the work begins. The feedback describes clear targets, defined deliverables and forward thinking — a project structure that other people can understand and align to.

  • Strategic planning and project preparation
  • Clearly stated targets and deliverables
  • Forward thinking and structured execution
Reported in 360 feedback: one reviewer described “remarkable clarity regarding project targets and deliverables”, alongside advanced planning before project initiation.
04Engineer

Understanding is translated into practical engineering. The feedback describes strength across architecture, scripting and platform work — engineering that is designed to run in production, not just to demonstrate a concept.

  • Architecture and technical design
  • PowerShell and automation engineering
  • Windows and platform engineering
  • SRE practice and operational tooling

06 — Leadership

Leadership Without Dependence on Hierarchy

The feedback describes leadership expressed through technical credibility, capability building and influence — demonstrated through what is delivered and what others become able to do.

01

Lead by Solving

Demonstrate practical solutions. Credibility is built by solving the problem in front of the team, in production, with something that works.

02

Lead by Teaching

Share knowledge rather than keeping expertise concentrated. Advice, troubleshooting support and technical guidance are given generously.

03

Lead by Documenting

Make knowledge accessible. Documentation is produced frequently and at a high standard so that understanding is not locked inside one person.

04

Lead by Enabling

Create tools and automation that help others work faster and more consistently — capability that is handed over, not retained.

05

Lead by Connecting

Connect people, teams and expertise. Reviewers describe Den directing colleagues to the right specialists rather than holding the bottleneck.

Technical leadership does not always require formal authority. It can also emerge through expertise, trust, reliability, knowledge sharing and the ability to make other people more effective.
